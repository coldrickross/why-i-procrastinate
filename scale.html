The idea

How the scale works

Every time you decide whether to do something, your brain runs a quiet comparison. It piles every potential reward on one side and every possible cost on the other, then goes with whichever side is heavier.

If you're not taking action, the scale just isn't tilted toward action yet. It's not a character flaw. It's arithmetic: the costs side is carrying a bit more weight right now.

So the question stops being why am I so lazy? and becomes: what's on each side of my scale, and how do I change it?

Add weight to rewards. Take weight off costs. That's the whole game.

Same person, same legs

Three quick examples

Someone asks you to run a marathon tomorrow. You hate running. The discomfort of the run (the red weight) never changes. Only the other side does.

The only thing that changed was what's on the scale. That's what you're working with every single day.
